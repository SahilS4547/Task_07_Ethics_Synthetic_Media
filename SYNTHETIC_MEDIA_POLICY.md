# Responsible Synthetic Media Policy

## University Communications Office

### Policy Purpose

This policy establishes requirements for the creation, use,
publication, and management of synthetic media by a University
Communications Office.

Synthetic media includes AI-generated or materially AI-modified audio,
video, images, avatars, voices, or other representations that may
reasonably be interpreted as depicting human speech, appearance, or
behavior.

The purpose of this policy is not to prohibit useful applications of
synthetic media. It is to establish boundaries that allow legitimate
educational and communications uses while protecting consent,
authenticity, transparency, and institutional trust.


## 1. Scope

This policy applies to:

- University Communications employees
- Student employees working for the office
- Contractors
- External agencies
- Vendors producing media on behalf of the office

The policy applies whether synthetic media is intended for internal or
external distribution.


## 2. Core Standard

Synthetic media may only be produced when:

1. The intended use is legitimate.
2. The underlying information is factually accurate.
3. Required consent has been obtained.
4. The synthetic nature of the artifact is appropriately disclosed.
5. The artifact has completed the required review process.
6. The expected communication benefit outweighs the foreseeable risk
   of misunderstanding or misuse.


## 3. Permitted Uses

Subject to the requirements of this policy, synthetic media may be used
for:

- AI-literacy and educational demonstrations.
- Research-related communication.
- Generic synthetic presenters in instructional material.
- Internal training material.
- Prototypes that are not represented as authentic human recordings.
- Accessibility applications.
- Multilingual narration or communication.
- Low-risk informational material where synthetic representation does
  not create a misleading impression of human participation.

A permitted category does not automatically authorize publication.
Consent, disclosure, and review requirements continue to apply.


## 4. Prohibited Uses

The University Communications Office will not use synthetic media to:

- Fabricate a statement or action attributed to a real person.
- Clone an individual's voice without documented consent.
- Reproduce an identifiable person's likeness without documented
  consent.
- Create deceptive student, faculty, alumni, or employee testimonials.
- Falsely imply endorsement by a real individual.
- Impersonate university leadership.
- Create synthetic emergency or public-safety announcements presented
  as recordings of real officials.
- Misrepresent research findings.
- Conceal the synthetic nature of realistic AI-generated
  communications.
- Create media intended to deceive audiences about whether a real
  person participated.
- Produce content where meaningful disclosure cannot reasonably be
  maintained and the risk of mistaken authenticity is substantial.

These uses remain prohibited even when the proposed creator believes
the communication would benefit the university.


## 5. Consent Workflow

The use of an identifiable person's voice, face, likeness, or other
recognizable characteristics requires explicit documented consent
before synthetic-media production begins.

Consent documentation must identify:

- The individual providing consent.
- The characteristics being reproduced.
- The purpose of the synthetic artifact.
- The intended audience.
- Planned distribution channels.
- Whether editing is permitted.
- Whether future reuse is permitted.
- The period for which consent remains valid.

The Communications Office must retain this documentation with the
project record.

Individuals must have a process for withdrawing authorization for
future uses.

Withdrawal does not guarantee removal of copies already distributed
outside university-controlled systems, and this limitation must be
explained when consent is obtained.

Public availability of photographs, recordings, speeches, or videos
does not constitute consent for synthetic reproduction.


## 6. Disclosure Standards

Realistic synthetic media intended for an audience must contain clear
disclosure.

For video, disclosure should appear visibly in the media itself and,
where appropriate, in accompanying text.

For audio-only media, disclosure should be spoken at an appropriate
point or clearly provided through the interface or accompanying
description.

Disclosure should use understandable language such as:

"This media contains AI-generated audio/video."

Disclosure should not rely exclusively on fine print or metadata that
an ordinary viewer cannot see.

When technically practical, the disclosure should remain connected to
the artifact when it is downloaded or redistributed.


## 7. Provenance Requirements

Where supported by the production system, the Communications Office
should preserve available:

- Content credentials
- Provenance metadata
- Platform watermarks
- Generation records
- Original source material

Each approved project should maintain an internal record containing:

- Generation tool used
- Date of generation
- Responsible employee
- Source material
- Final approved artifact
- Consent documentation
- Approval record
- Disclosure method
- Major edits or revisions

Visible disclosure must still be used when appropriate even when
technical provenance mechanisms exist.

Provenance is an additional safeguard, not a replacement for
transparency.


## 8. Factual Verification

Information communicated through synthetic media must meet the same
accuracy standards as equivalent human-produced university
communications.

AI generation must not be treated as verification.

Before publication, factual claims should be reviewed against the
appropriate source material.

Synthetic representation must never be used to make an uncertain claim
appear more authoritative.


## 9. Review and Approval

Externally distributed realistic synthetic media must receive review
before publication.

The reviewer should evaluate:

1. Is the proposed use permitted?
2. Is the underlying information accurate?
3. Has required consent been documented?
4. Is the disclosure clear?
5. Is provenance information preserved where possible?
6. Could a reasonable audience misunderstand who actually participated?
7. Could the artifact create significant harm if removed from its
   original context?
8. Is synthetic generation necessary or is conventional media a safer
   alternative?

High-risk or ambiguous projects should be escalated for additional
review rather than automatically approved.


## 10. High-Risk Communications

Additional scrutiny is required for synthetic media involving:

- University leadership
- Admissions
- Financial information
- Health or safety information
- Disciplinary matters
- Legal communications
- Research claims
- Crisis communications
- Public controversies

Synthetic representation should not be used merely because it is
faster or less expensive when doing so would materially increase the
risk of audience confusion.


## 11. Incident Response

If university-produced synthetic media is altered, misused,
misattributed, stripped of disclosure, or distributed in a misleading
context, the Communications Office should:

1. Preserve evidence of the incident.
2. Identify the affected artifact.
3. Notify appropriate university personnel.
4. Assess potential audience harm.
5. Publish clarification when necessary.
6. Contact relevant platforms when correction or removal is
   appropriate.
7. Document the response.
8. Determine whether the incident resulted from a policy or process
   weakness.
9. Update procedures when necessary.

The goal of incident response is both to address the immediate problem
and reduce the likelihood of recurrence.


## 12. Refusal to Produce

The University Communications Office should refuse to produce synthetic
media when:

- Meaningful consent cannot be established.
- Factual accuracy cannot be adequately verified.
- Appropriate disclosure cannot reasonably be provided.
- The artifact creates an unacceptable risk of mistaken authenticity.
- The proposed use involves prohibited impersonation.
- The communication concerns an area where authentic human presence is
  essential to trust or accountability.
- The potential harm exceeds the communication benefit.

The technical ability to generate an artifact does not create an
obligation to produce it.


## 13. Training and Review

Employees involved in synthetic-media production should receive
periodic training on:

- Consent
- Disclosure
- Provenance
- AI limitations
- Verification
- Incident reporting

This policy should also be reviewed periodically because
synthetic-media capabilities and associated risks will continue to
change.


## Conclusion

Synthetic media can support education, accessibility, research, and
communication, but realistic representation introduces risks that do
not exist in ordinary automated content.

Responsible use therefore requires more than disclosure alone.

Truthfulness, consent, provenance, human review, organizational
accountability, and the willingness to refuse inappropriate uses are
all necessary components of responsible synthetic-media governance.
