# Research Task 7: The Ethics of Synthetic Representation

## From Building It to Governing It

Research Task 7 continues the synthetic-media work completed in
Research Task 6.

Task 6 focused on constructing and evaluating synthetic media. I
created a D-ID talking-avatar video and an ElevenLabs synthetic audio
narration using generic or stock synthetic identities rather than
impersonating a real identifiable person.

Task 7 moves from production to governance.

The project examines what changes ethically when synthetic
representation is altered across four dimensions:

- Truth
- Consent
- Context
- Scale

It then translates those observations into a practical organizational
policy for responsible synthetic-media use.

## Why I Chose a University Communications Office

A university communications environment presents a useful governance
case because it combines legitimate opportunities for synthetic media
with situations where authenticity is especially important.

Synthetic media could support educational demonstrations, research,
accessibility, multilingual communication, internal training, and
low-risk informational content.

At the same time, universities communicate about admissions, research,
health, safety, leadership, and other areas where audiences need to
understand who actually made a statement.

This makes the environment appropriate for examining not only when
synthetic media should be allowed but when an organization should
refuse to use it.


## Reflection

The most surprising lesson from Task 6 was not simply the quality of
the generated media. It was how accessible the capability had become.

The D-ID video and ElevenLabs narration were not indistinguishable from
human performances under careful inspection. The video contained
repetitive facial movement and limited emotional expression, while the
audio contained unusually controlled pacing and reduced natural
breathing.

However, both could initially appear convincing to someone encountering
only a short portion of the artifact.

That observation changed how I approached Task 7.

Synthetic media does not need to be technically perfect to create
ethical concerns. Context, attention, distribution, and audience
expectations matter.

The transition from Task 6 to Task 7 therefore changed my focus from:

"Can this artifact be generated?"

to:

"Under what conditions should an organization generate it at all?"

My conclusion is that disclosure is necessary but insufficient.
Responsible synthetic-media use also requires truthfulness, meaningful
consent, provenance, review, accountability, and a willingness to
refuse uses whose risks cannot be adequately controlled.


## Ethical Scope

This repository contains written ethical analysis and governance work.

It does not create or upload new synthetic media depicting a real,
identifiable person.

The synthetic-media experiments referenced here remain documented in
the Task 6 repository.
